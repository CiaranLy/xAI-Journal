Week One -

Week Two -
*week two is issue one*
In Week Two, we started researching potential topics. Several papers were found and we decided to research into them. The different topics we chose to research were:
Ciaran - Topic 2 Viability Assessment: LIME/SHAP Disagreement
Cian - Topic 3: Is Chain-of-Thought a Reliable Explanation?

Aoibheann - Topic 4: Plausibility versus faithfulness
Everyone wrote a review on the papers that we looked into for choosing the topic. Once we had read these papers, we set aside time in the next week for a meeting to choose which one we were going to do.


Week Three -
In Week Three, we came together to choose to a topic.We each read out the different topics we had looked into,and compared 1. Was there enough depth in the topic 2. Is there enough research out there on this topic 3. What does the research say from what we've read and looked into. We landed on: How Saliency Explanations Are Evaluated in Medical Imaging. The reason we decided to do this topic is because not only is it relevant to the work we were doing in the course, but it was also a topic that interested us as well 

Week Four -
*week four is issue 3*
In Week Four, we began looking at different papers for our research topic. We each chose two papers to read from a list of papers, and then we discussed them.
Rosie - Looked into XAI in medical imaging and put together the initial list of papers for us to choose from.
Cian - "Seeing Isn't Always Believing: Analysis of Grad-CAM Faithfulness and Localization Reliability in Lung Cancer CT Classification" and "A User-Centric Analysis of Explainability in AI-Based Medical Image Diagnosis".
Ciaran - "Explainable AI: A Combined XAI Framework for Explaining Brain Tumour Detection Models" and "Self-eXplainable AI for Medical Image Analysis: A Survey and New Outlooks".
Aoibheann - “Towards a Transparent and Interpretable AI Model for Medical Image Classifications” and "CX-ToM: Counterfactual Explanations with Theory-of-Mind for Enhancing Human Trust in Image Recognition Models".

We discussed what claims the papers made, and how good and reputible of a source they were. We then discussed what type of paper we should do - a critique, a survey, or perhaps a new experiment. We put together all the research we had and what claims each paper made and started to form our thesis. 

Week Five -
*week five is issue 4*
In Week Five, we focused on creating the powerpoint. Ciaran divided up the presentation evenly between all of the group. The topics each person did in the slides were:
Rosie - "Topic and motivation"
Ciaran - "How the methods work, and how papers use them"
Cian - "Do the explanations hold up?"
Aoibheann - "How it fits into the content covered"
We created a slide deck and a script for the slides.

Week Six -
*week six is issue 5*
In week six, we presented our slides on the Monday. We presented them in front of the lecturer and the TA. After we were finished presenting, we had a meeting to discuss the paper. We discussed the headings, what information we felt would be needed in the paper and who would do what section. Ciaran also began doing an experiment for our paper.
For the experiment, Ciaran used brain MRI images to test four different explanation methods. He checked whether the heatmaps matched the tumour and what happened when the model was changed or parts of the image were removed. The results showed why we could not judge an explanation just by how the heatmap looked. This gave us an example to use alongside the papers we had read. Ciaran added the code, results and figures to the repo and posted an update in [Issue #6](https://github.com/CiaranLy/xAI-Journal/issues/6).

Week Seven - 
In week seven, we finished off the paper. Everyone combined their pieces and then as a group, we discussed what needed to be cut down and changed.
