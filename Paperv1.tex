\textbf{Point:1} Saliency maps are the dominant form of explanation in medical imaging AI
 
Evidence: Survey of 221 medical XAI papers: most use visual methods highlighting influential regions. In practice: Grad-CAM, LRP, SHAP, input gradients. 88\% of 33 surveyed physicians agreed it matters that AI explains its diagnosis.
 
Link - But what is the heatmap actually being claimed to show?
 
\textbf{Point2} - A heatmap supports two claims that are rarely separated: faithfulness (reflects what the model computes) and plausibility (matches where a clinician would look).
 
Evidence - Ma et al.: one method at mean rank 4.7 of 13 for plausibility but 8.4 for faithfulness, rank 1 best; (\textbf{how do I explain this unintuitive ranking but still cite it right?}) they conclude plausibility does not guarantee faithfulness. Our experiment reproduces this.
 
Evidence: Wagner: shown one correct and one incorrect AI diagnosis per format, 50\% of physicians trusted the false ones, heatmaps producing more false diagnoses than any other format.
 
Explain - Only faithfulness proves `the model learned clinically relevant features.'' Plausibility is a statement about human expectation, which is satisfiable by a model that is right for the wrong reasons.
 
Link - So a paper claiming faithfulness owes the reader a faithfulness test.
 
\textbf{Point 3}- Those tests are seldom run.
 
Evidence --1.Panboonyuen defines localisation accuracy, perturbation faithfulness and explanation consistency as equations, reports no value for any, and concludes from ten example images. 2.McGonagle treats agreement among Grad-CAM, LRP and SHAP as corroboration, though methods can agree and all be insensitive to the model.
 
Explanation -This is a pattern about what gets reported, not about what authors believe. We are not claiming post-hoc explanation is worthless, or that self-explainable models are the only route - the objection is to gathering evidence for one claim and asserting the other.
 
Link -if the problem is reporting, the solution is a reporting standard!
