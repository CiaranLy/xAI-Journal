\section{Introduction}

\textbf{Point1:} Saliency maps are the dominant form of explanation in medical imaging AI, yet the evidence on whether they help is contradictory.
 
Evidence: A survey of 221 medical XAI papers found most Medical Imaging AI models use visual methods highlighting influential regions as the method to explain the model [1]. Adoption remains limited, with 63\% of surveyed physicians reporting no experience of AI-based diagnosis [1], and the literature splits, Hatherley defending post-hoc explanation [2] while Kaufman and Kirsh argue post hoc XAI methods dont match how human experts build and communicate a diagnostic case [3].
 
Explanation: The field converged on heatmaps for practical reasons (cheap, post-hoc, model-agnostic), not because their value was settled.
 
Link: Why that argument has not resolved is the more useful question.
 
\textbf{Point2.} The literature disagrees because explanation quality is model- and task-specific. No ranking of methods can therefore fix the field, but the evidence each paper reports can.
 
Evidence: Panboonyuen finds Grad-CAM's fidelity degrades for Vision Transformers while holding for CNNs, and calls for model-aware interpretability [4]. Ma finds rankings vary by metric and dataset, with methods strong on one dimension weak on another [5]. Hou's survey separates evaluation into dimensions serving different purposes [6].
 
Explanation: ``Which method is best?'' has no answer that transfers. What transfers is evidence, letting each reader judge for their own model and task. That requires separating two claims: faithfulness, that the map reflects what the model computes, and plausibility, that it matches where a clinician would look.
 
Link: So the question is what evidence papers actually report.
 
\textbf{Point3.} Papers claim faithfulness and show plausibility.
 
Evidence: Panboonyuen defines localisation accuracy, perturbation faithfulness and explanation consistency as equations, reports no value for any, and then concludes from ten example images [4]. McGonagle treats agreement among Grad-CAM, LRP and SHAP as corroboration [7], though methods can agree and all be insensitive to the model [8]. Ma places one method at mean rank 4.7 of 13 for plausibility but 8.4 for faithfulness, where rank 1 is best [5]. (**CHANGE wording)
